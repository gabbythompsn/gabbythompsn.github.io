Explore the world through your plate

World cuisine is a vibrant reflection of geography, culture, and trade. From the delicate, seasonal precision of Japanese sushi and ramen to the hearty, sun-drenched flavors of Italian pasta and risotto, and the dynamic balance and textural harmony of Chinese cuisine, where stir-frying, steaming, and braising transform diverse regional ingredients into layered expressions of sweet, sour, salty, bitter, and umami, food tells the story of how people interact with their environment.

As globalization increases, fusion cooking has become more common, yet the "mother cuisines" remain the bedrock of culinary education. Modern chefs often look back at traditional techniques to create innovative new dishes that honor their heritage while embracing the future.

Japanese cuisine, or *washoku*, was added to the UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage list in 2013, emphasizing its focus on harmony and natural ingredients. Meanwhile, Italian cuisine has spread so widely that it is often considered the most popular international food, known for its reliance on high-quality, simple ingredients like olive oil, tomatoes, and basil. Chinese cuisine, one of the world’s oldest continuous culinary traditions, is celebrated for its emphasis on balance, regional diversity, and masterful technique, reflecting a deep integration of philosophy, climate, and centuries of cultural exchange.

  • Japanese cuisine: Focus on Umami and presentation.
  • Italian cuisine: Focus on simplicity and regionality.
  • Chinese cuisine: Focus on balance and harmony of texture
